# Problem 1. Prove that $\sqrt{2} + \sqrt{3}$ is irrational.
1. Prove by contradiction: assume $\sqrt{2}+\sqrt{3}$ is rational.
2. That would imply there exists some $A, B \in \mathbb{Z}$ such that $\sqrt{2}+\sqrt{3}=\frac{A}{B}$ and $\frac{A}{B} \in \mathbb{Q}$

$$
\begin{equation}
\begin{split}
\sqrt{2}+\sqrt{3}&=\frac{A}{B}\\
B(\sqrt2+\sqrt3)&=A\\
B^2(2+2\sqrt6+3)&=A^2\\
B^2(5+\sqrt{24})&=A^2\\
5+\sqrt{24}&=\frac{A^2}{B^2}\\
\sqrt{24}&=\frac{A^2}{B^2}-5\\
\end{split}
\end{equation}
$$

3. Because $\mathbb{Z}$ is closed under multiplication, $A,B\in\mathbb{Z} \implies A^2,B^2 \in \mathbb{Z} \implies \frac{A^2}{B^2} \in \mathbb{Q}$
4. Because $\mathbb{Q}$ is closed under addition, $\frac{A^2-5B^2}{B^2} \in \mathbb{Q}$
5. So, we know _RHS_ is in $\mathbb{Q}$.
6. However, we know that $\sqrt{24}$ is irrational. $\sqrt{24}\notin\mathbb{Q}$
7. So, we know _LHS_ is not in $\mathbb{Q}$.
8. This is a contradiction. Hence, $\sqrt{2}+\sqrt{3}$ cannot be rational.
9. Therefore, $\sqrt{2}+\sqrt{3}$ must be irrational $\blacksquare$
