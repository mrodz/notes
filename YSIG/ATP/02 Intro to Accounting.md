## Types of Accounting
### Financial Accounting
Focuses on reporting financial information to external stakeholders (eg. investors, creditors, regulators)
Bound by formal reporting requirements

## Managerial Accounting
Focuses on data for internal use, helps with decision making, planning, and performance evaluation
Not bound by formal requirements

## GAAP (Generally-Accepted Accounting Principles)
- Accounting rules in the USA
- Other countries follow other rules (usually IFRS)
- Goal is to standardize financial reporting so that finances are easily comprehensible and comparable
### Key Assumptions
- Accounting Entity
- Going concern - companies are assumed to remain in existence indefinitely
- Measurement - must represent things that are measurable
- Periodicity - companies are required to file annual, quarterly, interim, reports

### Historical Cost Principle
Financial statements report companies' resources at an initial historical cost
#### Example
If a company buys a new plot of land for $1M and 5 years later, the land is valued at $5M, the company's financial statement would still report the value of the land as $1M.

#### What about Intangible Assets?
These are non-physical property of a company that helps generate money nonetheless (eg. patents, customer information, trademarks, etc.)

#### Example 1
Company A buys a set of customer information from a database for **$1M**. Its financial statements would represent an intangible asset of **$1M**.

#### Example 2
Over the years, company A has collected a ton of customer information. They estimate its worth about **$1M**. In fact, company B has offered to pay them **$1M** for the information, although Company A wants to keep it private. The financial statements would <u>not</u> represent this asset.

### Accounting Principles 2 & 3
#### Revenue Recognition Principle
A company records revenue when it is earned and measured.

#### Matching Principle
Costs associated with making a product or delivering a service must be recorded during the same period as revenue generated from that product or service.

#### Cash Accounting vs Accrual Accountings
Cash accounting: revenue is recognized when cash is received and costs are recognized when cash is paid.

#### Example 1: Revenue Recognition
Scenario: Magazine co. runs 3-year promo subscription to magazine

Inventory is not recognized as cost, it goes to the cash flow statement not income statement

## Three Statements
### Income Statement
Shows a company's revenue, and costs, over a period of time on an accrual basis
Should something go on the income statement? **Only if a service has been provided**
Top Line = Revenue
Bottom Line = Net Income, which flows into...
### Cash Flow Statement
Connects a company's accrual financial activities over a period of time to cash
"Actual money changing hands"
Bottom Line = change in cash, which flows into...
### Balance Sheet (a snapshot in time)
Outlines what a company owns (assets) and owes (liabilities) at a point in time.
Assets: Cash, Buildings, etc.
Liabilities: Debt, Leases, etc.

You always want assets to more than cover your liabilities, as whatever remains goes to shareholders

Depreciation shows up on the income statement and the cash flow statement
