- The balance sheet reports the company's resources (assets) and how those resources were funded (liabilities and shareholders' equity) on a particular date (end of quarter/end of year)
- Contrast with income statement, which reports a company's revenues, expenses, and profitability over a specific period of time

> Liabilities are generations of capital that we have to pay off

$$\text{Assets}=\text{Liabilities}+\text{Equity}$$
## Assets
Represent a company's resources. To qualify as an asset, the following requirements must be met:
1. Company must own the resource
2. The resource must be of value
3. The resource must have a quantifiable, measurable cost

## Key Line Items
### Current Assets

| Name                  | Description                                                                                                             |
| --------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Cash                  | Money held by the company in its bank accounts                                                                          |
| Marketable securities | Debt or equity securities held by the company                                                                           |
| Accounts receivable   | Payment owed to a business by its customers for products and services                                                   |
| Inventories           | Any unfinished or finished goods waiting to be sold, and the direct costs associated with the production of these goods |
| Prepaid expenses      | When a company prepays for things like utilities, insurance, and rents, the right to future services become assets      |
### Long-term assets
| Name                               | Description                                                                                                                                         |
| ---------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Property, Plant & Equipment (PP&E) | Land, buildings, and machinery used in the manufacture of a company's services and products                                                         |
| Intangible assets & goodwill       | Non-physical assets such as patents, trademarks, and goodwill acquired by the company that have value based on the rights belonging to that company |

## Liabilities and Equity
- Represent the company's sources of funds (how it pays for assets)

### Liabilities
1. Must be measurable
2. Their occurrence is probable
3. Examples
	1. Accounts payable: A company's obligations to suppliers for services and products already purchased for them, but which have not been paid
	2. Accrued expenses: Employee compensation that the company has incurred, but for which it has not yet been paid
	3. Short-term debt <=12m
	4. Long-term debt: >12m
### Equities
1. Equity investment
2. Retained earnings (what the company has earned through operations since its inception)
3. Examples
	1. Preferred stock: stock that has special rights and takes priority over common stock
	2. Common stock: represents capital received by a company when it issues shares
	3. Treasury stock: Common stock that had been issued and then reacquired (bought back) by a company
	4. Retained earnings: Total company earnings/losses since its inception, minus all dividends

Liabilities for apple
- Funds owed to suppliers: yes
- Apple's long term debt: yes
- Office expenses generated during the period, if they have already been paid: no
- Office expenses generated during the period, if they have not been paid: yes
- Depreciation of assets: no
- Dividends owed to shareholders: no

Why invest in equity if liability holders are higher on the chain? Because there is a higher reward investing in equity.

## Several items on the Balance Sheet are related to COGS
1. Inventory: when a company sells inventory its balance of inventory decreases and a COGS expense is recognized of equal value
2. Labor: salaries are paid in cash. Cash goes down, COGS expense is recognized of equal value
3. PP&E: Depreciation decreases the value of PP&E on the balance sheet. Some of this expense is recognized on the income statement

Cash equivalents are extremely liquid assets: US T-notes with a term <= 90 days
Marketable securities (debt or equity investments held by the company) included in this line item or broken out separately.

## PP&E are long term assets used to generate income over multiple years; PP&E is presented net of depreciation
- Presented at minimum of cost and market value, if it is "written down" it is an expense on the income statement (usually below operating income as its own line item)
- If a company decides to sell an asset, the PP&E balance is removed and replaced with any cash received and a gain/loss is recognized on the income statement

## Goodwill
The amount by which the purchase price for a company exceeds its fair market value (FMV), representing the "intangible" value stemming from the acquired company's business name, customer relations, employee morale. Note: Goodwill is effectively an accounting plug, created only if the purchase price if higher than assets.

## Accrued expense
Wages, insurance, rents, taxes, dividends, litigation costs that have already been incurred but not yet paid.

## Deferred revenue (backlog)
Revenue received for services not yet provided by the company
- Sizable liability for software companies (subscription services) and companies that sell long term memberships like magazine subscriptions and gift certificates

## Retained Earnings
$$\text{Retained earnings (ending)}=\text{Retained earnings (beginning)}+\text{Net income}-\text{Dividends paid}$$

## Market Value vs. Book Value
1. Book value: the value of assets or equity on the balance sheet does not represent market value
2. Market value is almost always WAY higher than book value
	1. Market cap is a measure of the market value of equity, book value of equity is essentially useless for analysis purposes
