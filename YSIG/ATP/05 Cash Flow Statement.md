Provides an aggregate view of the inflows and outflows of cash into a business during the stated period. It is divided into 3 sections

1. Cash from Operations
	- How much cash did the company earn from doing their business?
	- Uses net income as starting point and converts accrual-based net income into cash flow operations via a series of adjustments
2. Cash from Investing Activities
	- Asset sales and purchases
3. Cash from Financing Activities
	- New borrowing/pay-down of debt/new issuance of stock/share repurchases

Without the cash flow, you don't know anything about the company's liquidity and cash position

The income statement shows a company's true profitability

Income statement might not accurately represent a company's liquidity and can be manipulated

Working capital = money tied up in business

## Warning
$$ \text{Net Income} + \text{D\&A} \neq \text{EBITDA} - \text{Interest} - \text{Tax Expense}$$

Usually the biggest adjustment to net income is adding back depreciation, for some types of companies, D&A is even larger than net income itself

## CFO: Working capital

$$ \text{CFO} = \text{Net Income} + \text{Depreciation} - \Delta \text{Working Capital} $$

- Current assets like inventories, accounts receivable, and prepaid expenses are called working capital assets
- Current liabilities like accounts payable, accrued expenses, and deferred revenue are called working capital liabilities
