## Major Line Items
| Line Item                         | Definition                                                                                                                                                                                                |
| --------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Net Revenue                       | Total $ payment for goods and services                                                                                                                                                                    |
| COGS                              | Represent a company's direct cost of manufacture (for manufacturers) or procurement (for merchandisers) of a good or service that the company sells to generate revenue                                   |
| Gross Profit                      | Revenue minus COGS                                                                                                                                                                                        |
| Selling, General & Administrative | Operating costs not directly associated with the production or procurement of the product or service that the company sells to generate revenue. Payroll, wages, commissions, meal and travel, stationary |
| Research and Development          | A company's activities that are directed at developing intellectual property.                                                                                                                             |
| EBITDA                            | Earnings before interest, taxes, depreciation & amortization: a popular measure of a company's financial performance - Gross profit minus SG&A minus R&D.                                                 |
| D&A                               | The allocation of cost over a fixed asset's useful life in order to match the timing of the cost of the asset when it is expected to generate revenue benefits.                                           |
| Operating Profit (EBIT)<br>       | Earnings before interest and taxes: EBITDA - D&A                                                                                                                                                          |
| Interest Expense                  | Interest expense is the amount the company has to pay on debt owed. This could be to bondholders or to banks. Interest expense subtracted from EBIT equals earnings before taxes.                         |
| Income Tax Expense                | The tax liability a company reports on the income statement                                                                                                                                               |
| Net income                        | EBIT - Net Interest Expense - Other Non-Operating Expenses                                                                                                                                                |
## Recall Accrual Accounting
A company records revenue when it is earned and measured

## COGS or COS
A company's direct cost of manufacture or procurement
I buy $100 worth of lemons for my lemonade stand I will run in two weeks. Even though I spent $100, since I haven't sold any cups of lemonade, I will NOT count it as COGS yet under the **MATCHING PRINCIPLE**

Example COGS: Inventory, Direct Labor, Factory Overhead, Shipping

Labor is a COGS for a factory or a mine
Labor is not a COGS for McDonalds (it is instead SG&A)

## R&D
Usually for research-intensive industries (healthcare, energy, tech). Includes compensation for employees, equipment, and facilities engaged in R&D process.

### Depreciation 
The matching principle: costs associated with making a product or delivering a service must be recorded during the same period as revenue generated from that product or service

### Intuition
You can think about Depreciation in two ways
1) OK: Assets break down over time - depreciation keeps track of that wear and tear
2) Better: 

Depreciation is not a line on the income statement. Its a component of COGS or SG&A depending on whether the asset is tied to manufacturing (cost of building a tire plant) or tied selling or marketing (cost of cash register)
Intangible assets are ONLY EVER REPRESENTED if they are bought externally. You can't arbitrarily assign value to intangible internal assets.
Goodwill is a line item on the balance sheet. Tricky and usually strictly governed by auditors

Stock-based compensation is like D&A because it is a non-cash expense, and its not its own line item on the income statement (its embedded within sales, general, and administrative expenses)

## Net Income
Referred to as the "bottom line" - income after you've removed (almost) all expenses from the business, including those not related to core operations
- The denominator of Price to Earnings
- The Numerator of EPS

It's important but gets much less attention in finance than you think... mostly because it is not comparable.

## EBIT / Operating Income
One of the key reasons for accounting is for comparison but not all metrics are equally useful to compare

A great comparison between being "low" on the income statement and highly comparable
- Excludes interest expense which can vary heavily depending on a company's capital structure (how much debt they have)

## EBITDA
"Ballpark Cash Flow" - Nate
"Bullshit Earnings" - Warren Buffet

Stripping out D&A makes it closer to operating cash flow
Removing Interest Expense makes companies more comparable
