## Perfect Competition
- A perfectly competitive market is one in which:
	- The good is homogenous
	- There are a very large number of sellers
	- There are a very large number of buyers
	- There is "free entry"
		- ie. No barriers to entry
- We can use Marginal Analysis to show a relationship between a firm's Supply Curve and its Marginal Cost curve.

### The Firm's Supply Curve
- Suppose MC is upward sloping
- Consider three different quantity choices
	- $p < MC(q_1)$ "less than what is costs to make"
	- $p>MC(q_2)$ "leaving profits on the table"
	- $p=MC(q_3)$ "equilibrium"
- What should the firm do?
	- Produce $q_3$
- You can imply that the Marginal Cost curve stands in for the Supply Curve
	- Later on, we will qualify this as saying: "The Supply curve is equal to that part of the Marginal Cost Curve that is above the Average Variable Cost Curve"

## Short Run and Long Run
- In economics generally, the short run is often used to mean "a period in time in which we are not in equilibrium"
	- ie. in a product market where there is excess supply or demand
- In Producer Theory, short-run more specifically is a "period of time during which one or more inputs of production cannot be changed"
	- Or equivalently, the period of time in which some costs are fixed

## Cost structure and price when $AVC(q)<p<ATC(q)$
$$\pi=(p-AC)q$$
As AFC goes to zero, the difference between ATC and AVC decreases (AFC is downward sloping)

Should firms operate if doing so would mean a loss?
- Not in the long run - alternative is zero profits, so why run a loss!
- But what about in the short run? The choice is between:
	- Operate and it earns a profit that is: $$\pi_{operate}=pq^*-VC-FC$$
	- Shutdown and profit is: $$\pi_{shutdown}=-FC$$
	- Shutdown condition: $$\begin{equation}\begin{split}\pi_{operate}&>\pi_{shutdown}\\pq^*&>VC\\p&>\frac{VC}{q^*}\\p&>AVC\end{split}\end{equation}$$
- Firms should operate in the long run if price is greater than the average variable cost

## Long Run Equilibrium
- Entry and exit
	- If $P>AC \implies \text{Profits} \implies \text{Entry} \implies P \downarrow$
	- If $P<AC \implies \text{Losses} \implies \text{Exit} \implies P \uparrow$
	- If $P=AC$ we are at equilibrium
- Fact 1 about PC: Always the case that firms choose $q^*$ such that $$p=MC(q^*)...(1)$$
- Fact 2 about PC: In equilibrium (no driver for entry or exit) $$p=AC(q^*)...(2)$$
- Equation $(1)$ and $(2)$ imply that: $$MC(q^*)=AC(q^*)$$
- This means $q^*$ is chosen such that Average Costs are at a minimum

### Perfectly Competitive Equilibrium is when $q$ is efficient
- Leads firms to produce where $AC$ is lowest
