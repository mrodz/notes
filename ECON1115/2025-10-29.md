## Profit Maximization
- Marginal analysis logic says a firm should produce until: $$MR(Q)=MC(Q)$$
- But in perfect competiton, $MR(Q)=p$, so that simplifies to: $$p=MC(Q)$$
- In a monopoly, marginal revenue is not price, so we need: $$MR(Q)=MC(Q)$$
![[images/Monopolistic Pricing.jpg]]

$$Q^*=\frac{a-mc}{2b}$$
$$P=\frac{a+mc}{2}$$
Recall: $$P+\frac{\Delta P}{\Delta Q}Q=MC$$
$$\frac{P-MC}{P}=-\frac{\Delta P}{\Delta Q}\times\frac{Q}{P}=-\frac{1}{\epsilon}$$
Called __Lerner-Index Markup__

## Natural Monopoly
- We assumed eventually average costs will rise
- Fixed costs of $F$ and marginal costs of $m$
$$C(Q
F+mQ$$
$$AC(Q)=\frac{F}{Q}+m$$
- Average costs converge to marginal costs of $m$
### Implications for costs
- A usual feature of competition is that it pushes $AC$ to their minimum
- When $AC$ always decreases as $Q$ increases, competition actually means that minimum costs are higher than they would be with one firm:
	- Natural monopoly is one in which cost-minimizing number of firms is 1
- But even in natural monopoly, does not mean that one firm is 'good'
	- Will have market power
## Natural Monopoly Policy approaches
- Do nothing
	- Could have competition that is "inefficient" in sense that costs are increased
	- Could have huge producer surplus
- Public Ownership
	- Government provides the good directly
- Regulated private ownership
	- Government controls the price to avoid high Deadweight Loss
