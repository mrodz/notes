### Income and Substitution Effect
$p_2$ decreases, two things happen
1. Consumer's purchasing power (aka real income) has increased
2. Good 2 cheaper relative to good 1

These lead to 2 effects
1. Substitution effect
2. Income effect

When analyzing these two after a change shift in BC, we graph a third BC that is a ghost (never physical)
1. It has the slope of the new BC, but...
2. It is tangent to the old IC

In the vast majority of cases, IE will be small unless we spend a large share of our budget on good
- A "Giffen" good is an inferior good in which the income effect is so strong that the good does not obey the law of demand

### What do firms aim to do?
- Economists typically assume that firms want to maximize profits
- Why do we think this is a reasonable assumption?
	- Darwinian pressure to make profits?
	- Publicly-listed firms have a fiduciary duty to shareholders

### Profit Maximization is the objective. But what do firms do?
- They produce outputs from inputs: $$Q=F(L)$$
	- Where $Q$ is output
	- $F(.,.)$ is a "Production Function"
	- $L$ is labor (for now, assume it's the only input)
- Subject to the costs of production: $$C=wL$$
- Profits: Revenue less Costs: $$\pi=pQ-C$$

### How do firms maximize profits?
- Let's assume we're in a perfectly-competitive market

### Marginal Product of Labor
MPL is the additional output produced when the quantity of labor used is increased by one unit.

$$MP_L=\frac{\Delta F(L)}{\Delta L}$$
### Marginal Revenue Product of Labor
The additional revenue produced when the quantity of labor used is increased by one unit
$$MRP_L=p\frac{\Delta F(L)}{\Delta L}$$

Let's use Marginal Analysis to find optimal $L$
- What is Marginal Benefit in dollars of using one more unit of labor?
	- One more unit of labor produces $MP_L$ units of output
	- So, one more unit generates $pMP_L$ units of revenue
- What is Marginal Cost of using one more unit of Labor?
	- Wage rate ($w$)
- So, $MB=MC \implies pMP_L(L^*)=w$
