## Utility Function
- Consider one good $x_1$.
- We want to know how much a consumer likes consuming diff quants of $x_1$
- The "__utility__" from consuming quantity $x_1$ is given by the following __utility function__: $$ U(x_1)$$
- For the moment, we are going to think of this as something measurable
	- We will drop this notion later on

## Utility Functions
Can have differing $\frac{\Delta Y}{\Delta X}$.

### How to calculate marginal utility
The slope of a utility function is its marginal utility.
If $U(x_1)=ln(x_1)$, then $MU(x_1)=\frac{1}{x_1}$

## Let's go back to a two-good example (a bundle of goods)
$$U(x_1, x_2)$$
It assigns the "utility" that the consumer gets from a bundle of goods

Comparing two bundles $(x_1, x_2) = (3, 2), (1, 4)$
- If $U(3,2)>U(1,4)$, then we prefer $(3,2)$
- If $U(3,2)<U(1,4)$, then we prefer $(1, 4)$
- If $U(3, 2)$ = $U(1,4)$, then we are indifferent between the two bundles

## Using Marginal Analysis to derive demand in the 2 good case
- Start at a random $(x_1, x_2)$ which exhausts the budget.
- Should you increase $x_1$ by one unit?
- What's the marginal benefit?
	- $MU_1$
- What's the marginal cost?
	- $\frac{P_1}{P_2}$
- Did increasing $x_1$ help us out? Only if $$MU_1 > MU_2 * \frac{P_1}{P_2}$$
- On the other hand, we should decrease $x_1$ if: $$MU_1<MU_2 * \frac{P_1}{P_2}$$
- And $x_1$ is just right if: $$MU_1=MU_2*\frac{P_1}{P_2}$$

## Optimal Consumption Rule
$$
\frac{MU_1}{MU_2} = \frac{P_1}{P_2}
$$
