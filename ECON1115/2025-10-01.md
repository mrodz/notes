## The curvature of Indifference Curves
Substitutes and complements
- Shape of IC's reveal information about relationship between goods
- More curved ICs describe goods that are more complementary to one another
- More straight ICs describe goods that are more easily substitutable for one another

## Income and substitution effects
1. When $p_2$ decreases, two things happen
	1. Good 2 has become cheaper relative to good 1
	2. Consumers' purchasing power (aka real income) has increased
2. These lead to two effects on consumer spending
	1. Substitution effect: change in choices resulting from a change in relative prices
	2. Income effect: change in choices resulting from a change in purchasing power
	3. Together these comprise the "Total Effect"

## Substitution and Income Effects and the Law of Demand
Consider a fall in price in $x_1$ in a good you purchase
- Let's think about the substitution effect
- The SE is the effect of $x_1$ being now cheaper than other goods
	- Ignoring the effect that purchasing power has gone up
- Substitution effect says that $\downarrow p_1 \implies \uparrow D_1$
- Now let's think about the income effect
	- The Income effect is the effect of you now being richer
	- It says that $\downarrow p_1 \implies \uparrow \text{Purchasing Power}$

## Recap
S.E. Says: $\downarrow p_1 \implies \uparrow D_1$ ALWAYS
I.E. Says: $\downarrow p_1 \implies \uparrow \text{Purchasing Power} \implies \uparrow D_1$ (if good is normal)
I.E. Says: $\downarrow p_1 \implies \uparrow \text{Purchasing Power} \implies \downarrow D_1$ (if good is inferior)

Hence, for a normal good, Income and Substitution effects go the same way
But, for an inferior good, Income and Substitution effects go opposite ways
