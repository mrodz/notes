- A tax will almost always reduce quantity, increase price to buyer, and reduce price kept by seller
- the reduction in surplus due to a tax is known as a __Deadweight loss__.
- Who hands the tax (bears imposition of the tax)
- Who bears burden (bears incidence of the tax)

For a given shift up in $S$, when $D$ is steeper:
- $P$ (inclusive of tax) goes up by more
- $Q$ goes down by less
- Incidence is more on the buyer
- DWL is smaller
- Tax Revenue is larger

## Incidence of Taxation - Intuition
- If consumer is $P$ insensitive, and really wants a good, they will pay almost any $P$. Eg. a life-saving drug
- If consumer $P$ is sensitive ($P$ goes up will just switch to something else)
- With a new tax, neither wants to bear the burden but somebody must
- Q: Who wins this game of chicken?

## Let's bring together Taxation and Elasticity to ask a normative question
- Remember that to evaluate any tax, we need to know its **Deadweight Loss**.
- Goods where demand is price inelastic (necessities/goods with no close substitutes) have small Deadweight Losses associated with taxation.
- So, focus on taxation on them?
	- We don't see this in the real world. There is a question of ethics abound.

## Marginal Analysis
- Scarcity leads to constraints on our decision making
- Marginal Analysis is a way of approaching constrained optimization
- Leading question: "__What is the optimal level of any activity (X)__"?
- Core idea:
	- Should you do a little more of this activity?
	- Less?
	- What is the balance for which we should strive
- In the abstract, think of the benefit of activity x as: $$B(x)$$
- ... and the cost of x: $$C(x)$$
- Net benefit: $$B(x)-C(x)$$
- Marginal Benefit: $$MB(x) = \frac{\Delta B}{\Delta x}$$

| x   | B(x) | MB(x) = ΔB/ΔX |
| --- | ---- | ------------- |
| 1   | 10   | 10            |
| 2   | 15   | 5             |
| 3   | 18   | 3             |
## Diminishing returns
- Normally, we think of the Extra benefit that one gets for any unit of input gets smaller as the number of inputs grows.
