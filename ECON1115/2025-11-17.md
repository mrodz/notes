## Expected Income of a "Bet"

- There are two "states of the world"
	- They occur with probability $p$ and $(1-p)$
- The "Bet" pays:
	- $Y_1$ with probability $p$ and $Y_2$ with probability $p_2=1-p$
- Expected income: $$E(Y)=pY_1+(1-p)Y_2$$
	- It is the weighted average of the returns with the weights being the probabilities of the states

### Example
- Bet pays:
	- 200M with probability $p_1=\frac{1}{10}$
	- 0 with probability $p_2=\frac{9}{10}$
- $E(Y)=.1(200)+.9(0)=20$

## Expected Profit - Risk
- Needs to pay a sunk cost of $10M
	- Works, earns $200M - $p_1=0.1$
	- Doesn't, earns $0 - $p_2=0.9$
- What is expected profit?
	- Recall: $\pi=\text{Revenue} - \text{Costs}$
	- Hence, expected profit = expected revenue - expected costs
	- Expected revenue = $20M 
	- Expected cost = $10M
	- Hence, $E[\pi]=\$10M$

## Expected Utility Theory
- Expected utility: weighted average of utility in each "state of the world"
- Expected utility theory: proposes people make decisions maximize expected utility
	- Note: highest expected utility, not highest expected income

## Expected Income v Expected Utility
- Expected Income: weighted average of income in each "state of the world" $E(Y)=pY_1+(1-p)Y_2$
- Utility of expected income (for certain): $$U(E(Y))=U(pY_1+(1-p)Y_2)$$
- Expected Utility is a weighted average of utility in each "state of the world"

## Risk Aversion
- An individual is risk averse if they prefer **Utility of Expected Income** over expected utility of income

## Empirical Example
- $U(Y)=\sqrt{Y}$
- State of the world 1: $10,000 with probability 0.25
- State of the world 2: $0 with probability 0.75
- Expected Income
	- $E[Y]=pY_1+(1-p)Y_2$
	- $0.25(10,000)+0.75(0)=2,500$
- Utility of Expected Income
	- $U(E[Y]) = U(2,500)=\sqrt{2,500}=50$
- Expected Utility
	- $E[U(Y)]=pU(Y_1)+(1-p)U(Y_2)$
