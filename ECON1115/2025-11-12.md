- Game theory studies strategic interactions among two or more actors
- A game has players, strategies, and payoffs
- A Nash Equilibrium of a game is a set of strategies when no player wants to unilaterally deviate from the strategy they are playing
	- Another way to say this: "Every player is playing their 'best response' to opponent's strategy"

## Cournot 'Game'
- Cournot duopoly has all the components of a 'game'
	- Players (the two firms)
	- Strategies (all the different quantity choices)
	- Payoff (profits)
- We want a Nash Equilibrium: a set of strategies ($q_1$, $q_2$) in which
	- No firm has an incentive to unilaterally deviate from choices

## The Residual Demand Curve facing Oligopolists

$$P=a-bQ$$
## Residual Demand and Marginal Revenue
- Linear Demand: $$p=a-bQ$$
- Linear Demand implies Marginal Revenue as follows: $$MR_1=p+\frac{\Delta p}{\Delta q_1}q_1$$
## We want a best response function

- As usual (e.g. for firm 1), set $q_1$ such that: $$\begin{equation}\begin{split}MR_1(q_1,q_2)&=MC_1(q_1)\\a-bq_2-2bq_1&=mc\\a-bq_2-mc&=2bq_1\\q_1&=\frac{a-bq_2-mc}{2b}\end{split}\end{equation}$$
- And for firm 2, $$q_2=\frac{a-mc}{2b}-\frac{1}{2}q_1$$
## Nash equilibrium: Algebra
So, firm 1 produces: $$q_1=\frac{1}{3}\frac{a-mc}{b}$$
Firm 2 produces the same, so the market quantity is:
$$Q^C=q_1+q_2=\frac{2}{3}\frac{a-mc}{b}$$

## Summary: how to find a Nash Equilibrium of this game
1. Find Firm 1's quantity as a function of $q_2$, using $MR_1(q_1,q_2)=MC_1(q_1)$
2. Find Firm 2's quantity as a function of $q_1$, using $MR_2(q_1,q_2)=MC_2(q_2)$
3. Find $q_1=q_2$

## Comparing Market Quantities
- PC: $$Q^{PC}=\frac{a-mc}{b}$$
- Cournot Duopoly: $$Q^{C}=\frac{2}{3}\frac{a-mc}{b}$$
- Monopoly: $$Q^{M}=\frac{1}{2}\frac{a-mc}{b}$$
$$Q^{PC}>Q^C>Q^M$$
AND
$$P^{PC}<P^C<P^M$$
