## Insurance

- A person has a house vulnerable to flooding
- Property value is $1,000,000
- A flood will happen this year with probability 0.01
- The utility they get is $\sqrt{H}$
- Consider an insurance contract
	- They invoice $12,000
	- If the flood happens, the insurer rebuilds the house
	- If the flood does not happen, the insurer does nothing

### With Insurance
| Flood Happens | Flood Doesn't Happen |
| ------------- | -------------------- |
| 988k          | 988k                 |
$$E[u]=0.99\sqrt{988k}+0.01\sqrt{988k}\approx 994$$
## Without Insurance
| Flood Happens | Flood Doesn't Happen |
| ------------- | -------------------- |
| 0             | 1,000k               |
$$E[u]=0.99\sqrt{1000k}+0.01\sqrt{0} \approx 990$$
So, it is better to buy insurance

But, look! The cost of insurance is $12,000 and the expected loss is $10,000 in the case without insurance. Why still pay? To maximize utility, since consumers don't necessarily want to minimize costs. Plus, a $1M loss would be so catastrophic that it doesn't make sense to subvert insurance.

## Which Pareto Efficient Outcome should society aim for?

- We can formalize our normative thinking about desirability of different market outcomes by using a Social Welfare Function
- Examples:
	- Suppose $U_i=\sqrt{Y_i}$. Two people (1, 2) and two distributions (A and B)
	- Utilitarian SWF is the sum of all individual utilities
		- $SW_{utilit}=u_1+u_2+u_3+\dots+u_N$
		- "It is the greatest happiness of the greatest number that is the measure of right and wrong" (Bentham 1776)
			- $Y_1^A=25, Y_2^A=36$
			- $Y_1^B=4, Y_2^B=64$
			- $U_1^A=\sqrt{25}=5$
			- $U_2^A=\sqrt{36}=6$
			- $U_1^B=\sqrt{4}=2$
			- $U_2^B=\sqrt{64}=8$
			- $SW^A=5+6=11$
			- $SW^B=2+8=10$
			- So, we'd pick Distribution A because it grants a higher social welfare, EVEN THOUGH distribution B has higher income. One hint is that A is more equal, B is more unequal
	- Rawlsian SWF attempts to maximize the utility of the least well-off
		- $SW_{Rawls}=\min[u_1, u_2, u_3, \dots, u_N]$
			- $Y_1^A=25, Y_2^A=36$
			- $Y_1^B=16, Y_2^B=100$
			- $SW^A=\min[\sqrt{25}, \sqrt{36}]=5$
			- $SW^B=\min[\sqrt{16},\sqrt{100}]=4$
		- We would pick distribution A because we have the highest social welfare. That's because behind Rawls's notion of a "veil of ignorance," we could be person 1, so we'd pick the most favorable distribution for person 1.

## Redistribution
- Consider a simple model with one richer and one poorer person
	- $Y_\text{richer}>Y_\text{poorer}$
- If we want to give \$1 to someone through taxation, someone else will loose \$$(1+d)$
	- There is a winner and a looser
	- What is the marginal benefit to the winner of the distribution? $$MU(Y_\text{poorer})$$
	- $\hat{Y}_\text{richer}=Y_\text{richer}-(1+d)t$
	- $\hat{Y}_\text{poorer}=Y_\text{poorer}+t$

## Government and Redistribution
- If a government has utilitarian preferences, think of it as a "social planner" that wants to achieve: $$(1+d)MU(Y_\text{rich})=MU(Y_\text{poor})$$
