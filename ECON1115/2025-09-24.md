	## Optimal Consumption Rule
Given:
$$
MU_1=MU_2\frac{p_1}{p_2} \text{ or } \frac{MU_1}{MU_2}=\frac{p_1}{p_2}
$$

Given:
$$
\frac{MU_1(x_1,x_2)}{MU_2(x_1,x_2)}=\frac{p_1}{p_2}
$$

We also know that the consumption choices use the entire budget:
$$
p_1x_1+p_2x_2=Y
$$
How many equations do we have? How many unknowns?

If we knew the functional form for utility/marginal utility, we could solve these two equations for the two unknowns $x_1^*$ and $x_2^*$.

## Example: Soda and Pretzels

$$
U(S,P)=\ln(S)+0.2P
$$
Let income be $14 and the price of soda and pretzels is $1, so we have:
$$
S+P=14 \implies p_s*S+p_p*P
$$
What are marginal utilities?
- For pretzels, you would be expected to work this out
$$
\begin{equation}
\begin{split}
\text{Let } S = \hat{S} \\
\\
&U(s=\hat{s}, p=1)= \ln(\hat{s}) + 0.2(1) \\
&U(s=\hat{s}, p=2)= \ln(\hat{s}) + 0.2(2) \\
\\
\text{Solve for }\frac{\Delta U}{\Delta P} &= 0.2
\end{split}
\end{equation}
$$
- For soda, as it is non-linear, it would be given to you. Hence, $MU(S)=\frac{1}{S}$

To find equilibrium:
$$
\frac{\frac{1}{S}}{0.2}=\frac{1}{1}
$$
$s^* = 5$ and $p^*=9$.

## Indifference curves
- A consumer is indifferent between bundles when they derive the same utility level from those bundles
- An indifference curve connects all consumption levels

## Slope of the indifference curve

When calculating slope of indifference curve, you want delta x_1 and x_2 to be the same

We want:
$$
\frac{Rise}{Run}=\frac{\Delta x_2}{\Delta x_1}
$$
How to calculate $\frac{\Delta x_2}{\Delta x_1}$ along the indifference curve?
- Increasing $x_1$ by $\Delta x_1$ and doing nothing else: utility increases by $\text{MU}_1\Delta{x_1}$.
	- But this will put me on a new indifference curve
	- To stay on the same indifference curve, we need to reduce $x_2$ by some quantity. _which?_.
- Given $\Delta x_1$, how much must you decrease $x_2$ to keep $U$ constant?
- Solve: $$ MU_1\Delta{x_1}+MU_2\Delta{x_2}=0$$
- To find: $$\frac{\Delta x_2}{\Delta x_1}=-\frac{MU_1}{MU_2}$$
- So, the slope of the curve is the negative of the ratio of the $MU$s.
	- Ie. the negative of the $MRS$. (marginal rate of substitution)
