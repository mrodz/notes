## Inequality and Latin America

Inequality is a classic feature of Latin American political and economic development.

One of the most unequal world regions by many metrics
- One of the highest Gini coefficients in the world
- Inequality in wealth replicated in access to health, education, and many other things.

## Poverty vs. Inequality
- Poverty is a measure of level of wealth or welfare
- Inequality is a measure of distribution of wealth or welfare

Inequality cannot be measured without referencing the broader group or society
- Societies with similar incomes have different distributions of resources
- May have important implications for poverty and welfare

Different ways of measuring inequality can produce different conclusions

## What distribution should we care about?
- Income
- Wealth
- Consumption
- Education/health
- "Opportunity"

Several common measures, each emphasizing a different aspect of inequality
- Gini coefficient
- Atkinson inequality measure

## Gini coefficient
Advantages:
- Simple to calculate, interpret, compare
- Can examine changes over time or differences within countries
- Has intuitive characteristics: doesn't matter who the low/high earners are, a transfer from a rich to a poor person will typically make the distribution more equal.

## Ratios
- Palma ratio: top 10% to bottom 40%

## Why care?
The poorest person in China today makes more than the average in 1970. If everyone is better off than they were, what's so bad?

## Relative deprivation
Welfare may depend not just on what a person has, but also on what those around them have. Some evidence in economics and psychology of a "cost" to being poorer than one's neighbors. Even if needs are met, people subjectively feel off.

## Inequality can hamper development
Places with high income or asset inequality may have a harder time with economic growth. 
- More individuals are poor
- Wealthy tend to consume less of their income
- May have unbalanced economic structure

Some evidence that inequality can lead to economic and political instability
- Increased disposable income tends to convert into investments and financial instruments, which can increase the damage of market crashes

## Social and Political Consequences
High levels of income/wealth inequality can have broader consequences on how society functions. 

Elites can use additional wealth to choose policies that benefit them and not society as a whole. 
- Low levels of taxation/redistribution
- High levels of corruption

These policies in turn can determine future distributions of wealth and welfare.

## Inequality of Opportunity
Many political philosophers argue that what we ought to care about is where there is inequality of opportunity.
