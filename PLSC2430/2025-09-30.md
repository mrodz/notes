## All "Good" or "Bad" Things Go Together?
- Virtuous and vicious circles between politics, economics, and endowments lead to different development trajectories
- Rich stay rich; poor remain poor
- "Only four types of economies in the history of the world: advanced, developing, Japan, and Argentina"

## The Argentina Paradox
In 1900, Argentina was one of the richest countries in the world.
- Richest country in Latin America
- Richer than every European country except for Britain
- Close to U.S. and Canada in terms of GDP/Capita

By the 1950s, Argentina had fallen behind
- History of political and economic instability
- Now lower than neighboring Chile/Uruguay

## How did Argentina come to be rich?
- Not especially wealthy during colonial era
- Light colonial rule
- Dependence on ranching economy
- Legacy
	- Tension between Buenos Aires and the interior
	- Tension between conservatives and liberals
- State-building hampered at Independence
	- "Weak" state, trade-oriented political development

## Argentina's Economic Miracle
- Annual growth rate of over 5%
- Propelled by export of grain and meat to Europe

## By WW1
- Population quadrupled
- Immigration transforms the ethnic composition and culture of the country with many long-term consequences 
- Similar income per capita to the West
- Increasingly urbanized population, rise of Buenos Aires ("The Next Paris")

## Juan Perón
Army general of middle-class background
- Alum of 1943 military coup
- Very popular vice president
- Sweeps 1946 presidential election

Nationalism, Populism, and Corporatism
- Attempts to channel wealth toward urban workers
- Highly state-directed economic policy
- Peronist party capitalizes on gains
- Strong growth until economic crisis

## Stop and Go Politics
- Rising discontent from military conservatives
- Increasing civil unrest
- Tensions rise with the Catholic church

Military sends Perón to exile
- Right-wing government cracks down on Peronist policies, officials, propaganda
- Attempted uprising by pro-Peronist forces brutally repressed
- New elections held, but specter of military intervention remains
- Ongoing difficulty of either co-opting or repressing Peronist support

## 2001 Crisis and Recovery
Overvalued exchange rate and high debt dependence lead to massive economic crisis
- Collapse of markets
- Default on debt
- IMF ceases payments following earlier bailout

Disastrous for Argentine economy and society

## Rise, Fall, Rise of "Kirchnerism"
- Néstor Kirchner wins presidency in 2003
	- Favors state intervention in economy, protectionism, and industrialization
- Succeeded by wife
	- Keeps his policies

## Theory 1: There is no puzzle
- Maybe Argentina was never as rich as it appeared.
- Physical and human capital lagged far behind other developed economies
- Very uneven distribution of wealth hampered investment
- Shortcomings
	- Other countries with high incomes but low education/investment managed to reform. Why not Argentina?
	- Other economies have experienced resource booms and busts, but few reached the heights of Argentina and then collapsed

## Theory 2: Bad luck
- Problems have to do with external shocks in global trade and capital markets
	- Argentina's advantage in fertile soil and agriculture declines as century continued

## Theory 3: Bad policies
- Budget deficits in response to populist pressures
- Reliance on protectionism

## Theory 4: Bad institutions
- "Weak-state" trap
