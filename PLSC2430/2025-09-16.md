## Poverty in Latin America

The problem of state weakness: "Latin American states, born weak, remained so decade after decade" (Munck and Luna, p.37)

State: a community that can control force and levy taxes on a substantial/permanent basis

State capacity: The *ability* of the state to impose political control, exert force, collect taxes, and impose policy.

## Causes and consequences of State "Weakness"
### Colonial institutions encouraged patrimonial practices
### Unable to build capacity after independence

## Marked differences in political trajectories
- Mexico: 20th century Revolution, construction of one-party state, eventual democratic transition
- Colombia: Power sharing among political elites allowed democracy tp survive, but long-running civil war in the countryside
- Argentina: The richest Latin American country at the beginning of the 20th century, and cycles of political and economic uncertainty
- Venezuela: The strongest democracies during the md 20th century, but plagued today by economic collapse

## What is poverty, really?
- "Having too few resources or capabilities to participate fully in society" (Smeeding, Oxford)
- "The minimum level of subsistence needed to ensure survival" (Rowntree, Poverty and Progress)
- "People lack the resources to... have the living conditions and amenities which are customary... in the societies to which they belong" (Townsend)

## Standard Poverty Measures
- Aggregate measures (GDP, GNI): simple to calculate but ignores distribution
- Headcount poverty:
	- Number of households/individuals living below the income threshold
	- Better measure of assessing number of people without access to resources

## The poverty line varies
- The world bank (2025) states that
	- Extreme poverty: less than $3/day
	- Lower middle income poverty line: less than $4.20/day
	- Upper middle income poverty line: less than $8.30/day

## Countries have other definitions
- US 2025: $15,650/year (individual), or $32,150/year (family of four)

## Challenges of measuring poverty
- Differences in standard of living
	- Townsend's idea of poverty *relative* to current conditions
- Consumption vs. income
	- Informal labor force can be difficult to measure
	- Difficult to calculate the income of subsistence farmers
	- Access to savings, assets, insurance, credits can shield people from shocks
- Government and social programs
	- Should we include government programs and subsidies, which vary a lot by country?
	- Should we consider pre- or post-tax wealth?
- Poverty is not just about wealth/income, but about access to opportunities and the ability to have basic needs met.

## Multidimensional Poverty Measures
- Many organizations measure poverty using measures of living standards
- Examples:
	- UNDP: Human development index
	- Mexico: CONEVAL Poverty Index and Marginalization Index
	- Costa Rica: Index of Multidimensional Poverty
	- Electoral Democracy Index (Democracy Project)
- Challenge: Assessing what goes into the index and how to weigh different components.

## The Cuba Paradox
- Cuba has a high poverty rate but performs well on many social measures
	- Low infant/maternal mortality
	- High life expectancy
	- High education rate
- High percentage of the economy spent on social programs
- BUT most measures of political/social representation rate Cuba poorly.
	- High rates of emigration

Progress on Poverty: Dramatic decline in poverty in 2010-onward. Latin America looks very different today than it did in 2000. Strong middle class is an explanatory feature in many theories about development.
- Improve social capital

## Brazil's economic miracle
One of the fastest growing economies of the world between 2000 and 2010.
- Rare example of explosive growth without increasing inequality
- Huge + in social programs, reduction in poverty
- Credited with good macroeconomic management, sound trade policies, etc.

Fortunes change around 2014.
- Commodity prices plummet
- Political and economic crisis stalls growth
- Economy shrinks, poverty grows, per capita income declines

No longer hailed as example for the region.
