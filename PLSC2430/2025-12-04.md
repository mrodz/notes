## China and LATAM
- Growing influence of China in last two decades
	- Major player in the commodities boom of 2000s
	- Trade with LATAM grows to over $500 billion by 2024
- Growing direct foreign investment and loans
	- Chinese OFDI in LaTam: $8.5B
	- Growing financing from Chinese Development Bank and Export-Import Bank
	- Most South American countries taking part in the Belt and Road initiative: Domestic infrastructure investment program grown global under Xi
- Chinese focus on "South-South cooperation"
	- Diplomatic partnerships with a growing number of countries in Africa, Latin America, South Asia
	- Push to isolate Taiwan
- Security Intervention
	- China has become a bigger player in financing the military of Latin American countries
	- Venezuela remains the largest purchaser

## China and the United States in Latin America
- US Policymakers express concern about the growing influence of China
	- Perception that it stands to erode the dominance of the US in the region
	- But not a major foreign policy focus
- LATAM countries have taken different approaches
	- South America; more eager to work with Beijing in general
	- Greater pressure from the U.S. in Central America/Carribbean
	- Mexico: announces major tariffs against Chinese imports
	- Brazil: sharp reduction in U.S. exports and rise in Chinese exports
- Question of how the "Donroe" doctrine might change things
	- U.S. taking a harder line in the region with trade, military, and sanctions
	- Pushing countries into the sphere of Beijing?
	- Pushback to Donroe Doctrine more limited than first imagined

## Regional Interests and Cooperation
- Within the region:
	- Mercosur trade bloc
	- Central American Integration System (SICA)
	- Andean Community of Nations (CAN)
	- Efforts to expand integration around/across the Pacific through the Pacific Alliance and the Trans-Pacific Partnership
- Example: The Bolivarian Alliance of the Americas (ALBA)
	- Founded by Venezuela and Cuba in 2004 to counterbalance U.S. power in region
	- Grows to 8 members at the height of the "pink wave" in 2009
	- But countries withdraw (Honduras, Ecuador) or are suspended (Bolivia) as domestic politics shifts rightward
	- Today: Cuba, Venezuela, Nicaragua, and a few small Caribbean nations
