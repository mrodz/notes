## Historical Antecedents
- "Americas" - diverse even before the conquest
- Latin American societies grew out of mixtures of cultures
## Creation of National Identities
- Mexico, Peru, Bolivia, and other countries with large indigenous societies forged identities that grapple with indigeneity/mestizaje
- Latin American national imageries often depict the "people" as the result of Spanish and Indigenous intermarriage
- This masks complexities in the distribution of modern populations
	- Influx of Asian immigration
	- Influx of Afrocarribean population
## Politics of Identification
- Who should be considered "indigenous" vs. "mestizo" vs. "ladino"
- Difficulty of identification: case study in Bolivia
	- 2012 census showed that total Indigenous population declined from 62% to 41% since 2001. Demographers do not agree.
	- Possibly due to the wording of the question: Do you "identify" vs "belong"?
## Development and Indigeneity
- In most (all?) Latin American countries, poverty and marginalization are higher among Indigenous people
- Almost half of indigenous people live in cities
- Social challenges of unemployment and displacement
## Afro-Latinos/Afro-Descendants
- About 130 million people in Latin America claim some African heritage
- "Morenos" in Venezuela
	- Afro-descendant with lighter skin pigmentation
	- Differentiated from "afrodescendientes"
	- Hugo Chavez claimed to be a moreno
## European Immigration
- By WW1, about a third of Argentina's population was European-born
- About 45-50% of Brazilians identify as "white"
## From Recognition to Representation
- Deciding which groups "count" and how to define them is a political choice, and it has political consequences
	- Patterns of political mobilization shape responsiveness
	- Voters, parties, and institutions determine who to respond to and why
- What is the role of identity-based mobilization in politics?
