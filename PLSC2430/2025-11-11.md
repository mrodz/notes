## Contentious politics and regime crises
- People who want to change or enact policy have a variety of tools at their disposal

## What is different about working outside the system?
- Political institutions depend on self-enforcing expectations and norms
	- Will political rivals respect elections and terms of office?
	- Will political parties/actors continue to exist between electoral cycles?
	- How can I be sure my opponents will not cheat?
- When actors subvert the political system, the system itself can get thrown into question

## Political Contention
- Episodic: structured around discrete events
- Public: interactions between claims-makers and targets of claim occur in the open and are observable to others
- Collective: Actions involve several individuals coordinating for similar goods
- Involves government: As claimant, party to claims, object of claims
- Social protest: Mobilization of masses to enact political/social change

## Protests and electoral competition are not always substitutes
- Simplistic to consider institutional and non-institutional contestation in isolation from one another
	- Role of mass protest in democratization
	- Many social movements work in tandem with institutionalized political contestation

## Armed Rebellion
- Trejo's argument applies most directly to social movements, but arguments potentially applies to armed rebellion as well
- Elections
	- Provide an opening for sensitizing people to political goals
	- Precipitate elite splits
	- Raise costs of repression
- Uptick in mobilization and acts of violence in election years in democratic contexts (eg. Colombia, Honduras)

## Individual Motivations and Collective Resistance
- We often examine political resistance from a structural or organizational perspective
	- The goals, tactics, or activities of an NGO, a rebel group, a political party, or a social movement
	- Political threat or opportunity structures
	- Regime response: accommodation, repression, or neglect?
