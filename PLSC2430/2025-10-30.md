## How do ordinary people get their voices heard?
- Protests, ballot, letters, bureaucracy

## The fight for political rights
- Indigenous groups (and other ordinary people) have taken a variety of strategies to make their voices heard

## Escape/Opting out
- Some indigenous people have sought to exit, rather than try to resist state authority
- Continued efforts to remain isolated from society
- Efforts to secure autonomy through separation
- Though this has become more difficult, some groups remain outside the reach of the state

## Contentious Politics
- Umbrella term encompassing
	- Popular protests
	- Strikes
	- Rebellions
	- Revolutions
- Violent revolt or insurgency on the basis of ethnic identity
	- Tupac Amaru rebellion
	- Caste War in the Yucatan
	- Peruvian Civil Conflict

## Gender Comparison
- Most LaTam countries have quotas to ensure representation of women
	- Less Guatemala and Venezuela
- Differ in structure, effectiveness
- Few attempts to do something similar for indigenous groups

## Ethnic Politics in Latin America
- Many countries include political parties that mobilize on the basis of ethnic or other identity-based claims
- Latin American region looks different
- Ethnopopulism
	- Marry ethnic-based appeals with classic populist mobilization strategies
- Ethnic polarization is lower in Latin America than in other world regions
	- Myth of mestizaje

## Importance of Populism
- Rise of MAS/Evo Morales
	- An outsider party prior to Morales, MAS made anti-establishment appeals
	- Morales = symbol of party through unique style "Evismo"
	- Nationalization and anti-trade efforts attract support of those disillusioned with neoliberal reforms

## Where have Afro-Descendants received recognition
- Garífuna of Honduras, Guatemala
- Elsewhere, it has been difficult for Afro-Descendants to frame demands in "ethnic" terms
	- Difficulty grappling with social and economic racism/exclusion
