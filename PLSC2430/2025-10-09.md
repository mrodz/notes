## What comes after the third wave?
### Some reasons for optimism
- Few total democratic breakdowns in Latin America since 1980
- Most Latin American countries continue to be formally democratic

### Some reasons for pessimism
- Increase in the number of presidents who fail to finish their terms
- Stagnation in democratic consolidation in much of the region
- Rise in authoritarian populism in some countries
- Few but alarming examples of democratic decline or reversal
	- Venezuela, Nicaragua, (arguably Ecuador, Peru, Bolivia)
- Challenge of democratic backsliding

## Democratic Backsliding
- Haggard and Kaufman: "The incremental erosion of institutions, rules, and norms that results from the actions of duly-elected governments"
- Potential examples
	- Constitutional Hardball
	- Consolidation of power in the executive
	- Erosion of civil liberties, conditions that make democratic participation possible for segments of society

## Democratic Decline in Venezuela
- Hugo Chavez elected in 1998 with popular support
- Calls for Constituent Assembly to replace 1961 Constitution
	- Stripped congress of powers
	- Weakened potential opposition parties
	- Disbanded and replaced supreme court
	- Created new institutions that were easier to co-opt
- Other reforms further consolidate power
	- Changing electoral system to give ruling party an advantage
	- Increased state control over economy, media
	- Increased pattern of authoritarian control since 2012
- These moves had significant popular support
	- While country was divided, Chavez had important backing for making these changes
	- Was any of this democratic? When did democracy end?

## Nicaragua
- Daniel Ortega democratically elected in 2006
	- Elected by plurality with strong support from the poor
- Builds on popularity through reforms to combat poverty
	- Begins to restrict media, journalism
- Amends constitution in 2009 to allow for re-election
	- Runs and wins election in 2011 with 60 percent of the vote (fraud?)
- Opposition grows to strengthening power, but fragmented
	- Wins re-election in 2016 with wife as vice-president in election boycotted by opposition
- Increased reliance on hardline tactics to defuse opposition
	- Arrest, exile, disappearances of opposition leaders
	- Crackdown on protest
	- Banning religious and civil organizations

## Beyond Venezuela and Nicaragua
- Reasons for concern in other cases
	- Similar restriction of opposition parties in Ecuador, Bolivia
	- Similar conflict with the judiciary in Mexico
	- Arguable rise in authoritarian populism in Brazil, among others
	- Rising radicalism against opposition in many contexts
- Pattern that institutional changes are often gradual and popular
	- Groups disenchanted with democratic process back reforms
	- Difficult to pinpoint when regime stops being a democracy
- Is democratic backsliding the new version of democratic reversal?

## Conclusions
- Classic theories of democratization have some explanatory power, but can't explain everything about Latin American political development
- Model of governance in Latin America has undoubtedly changed
- Challenges for the future
