## Democracy and Dictatorship in LaTam
- Political transformation since 1970
- Nearly all countries in Latin America are full or partial democracies
- (But: some more recent worries about democratic erosion)

| Term      | Def                                                                                                                                                                                               |
| --------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Regime    | The form of government or system of government. It is the rules of the game that determine who effectively holds power in a society. A single state might be ruled by different regimes over time |
| Democracy | A regime in which the political leadership is held accountable to the people through regular elections.                                                                                           |
Democracies and dictatorships vary on many dimensions
- Who is given the right to vote or otherwise participate in politics
- How elections are arranged and regulated
- How resources are allocated between different societal groups and how the rights of different societal groups are respected under law
- How disagreements within society are handled

Munck and Luna highlight five criteria to define a democracy
1. Regular elections as the means of access to top political offices
2. All adult citizens have the right to vote
3. Citizens have the right to organize parties and run for office
4. Elections are free of fraud and threats of violence/retribution
5. Citizens have freedom of expression, association, and assembly; free access to information

## Honduras: The Coup of 2009
- Elected president Mel Zelaya was ousted from office by the Army and sent into exile
	- But the army acted on orders of th eSupreme Court after he refused to abide by a court ruling prohibiting him from holding a referendum on rewriting the constitution.
- Fits the description of a coup in many respects
	- Army acts to remove democratically-elected leader
	- Efforts to intervene met with repression
- Opponents say Zelaya himself was a threat to democracy because he refused to abide by the Constitution/rule of law

## The Collapse of Venezuelan Democracy
- Venezuela was often considered a shining example of democracy in Latin America
	- But high levels of inequality, concentrated poverty, political stagnation under Punto Fijo Pact
- Complications in 1980s and 1990s
	- "Caracazo" protests of 1989
	- Coup attempts in 1992 with alleged Cuban involvement
	- Election of Hugo Chávez in 1998
- Did Chávez destroy democracy?
	- Constitutional reform in 1999 eliminates many checks and balances in constitution, but passed by popular referendum
	- Attempts to consolidate political and social control
	- Elites resist reforms, but lower classes argue that institutional change was necessary

## Challenges of Democracy in Latin America
- Most Latin American countries today are classified as "democracies" under most measures
- Nearly all hold elections
- Many examples of peaceful and democratic transition
- Over instances of democratic breakdown rare since the 1980s
- Major departure from earlier eras of democratic instability

## Rigged elections and electoral fraud
- Regular elections are held by the rules heavily favor one party or candidate
- Excluding groups from participating in elections (as voters or candidates)
- Stacking voters in uncompetitive constituencies
- Shifting governance responsibilities to different (unelected) offices
